§ II — Six Transformations

Static documents that could become living tools.

Six specific transformations, each named against a document FMB already publishes. Each takes nothing away from the standards, the IP, or the authority. Each delivers two distinct benefits — one to the FNFMB member, one to the FMB internal team.

1

The Living FAL Drafter

From Sample FAL (DOCX) to interactive wizard
Today

A 60-page Word document. A Nation downloads it, opens it, redrafts to fit their governance, then sends drafts back and forth with counsel and FMB.

Living

A web wizard. The Nation answers 25–40 contextual questions (size, governance, council composition, fiscal year, existing policies, NFR status). The tool produces a complete, FMB-aligned FAL draft, footnoted to relevant FMS standards, with redline notes for unusual elections.

For the Member

Reduces FAL drafting from 8–12 weeks to 1–2 weeks for a typical Nation.

For FMB

Certification team reviews more consistent, standards-aligned drafts; legal review queue shortens dramatically.

2

The Living FPC Ratio Coach

From static page to interactive coach · Working demo enclosed
Today

A page on fnfmb.com describes the four ratios. The Nation calculates them offline, emails them to their auditor, and waits for interpretation.

Living

Inputs flow from the audit or by direct entry. The four ratios are computed, classified (green/amber/red), commented on individually, and synthesized into an overall picture. Certification readiness is framed honestly. Capacity Development team sees the same view across their cohort.

For the Member

A Nation understands its position and pathway in minutes, not weeks. Council briefings become trivially simple.

For FMB

Certification team has a real-time picture of cohort readiness. DMPPP-eligible Nations surface earlier.

3

The Living FMS Self-Check

From XLSX template (2017) to walkthrough
Today

An Excel workbook with the fourteen FMS Standards. The Nation works through it cell by cell, often without confidence on what evidence each row requires.

Living

A web walkthrough of all fourteen standards. Each row asks 2–3 plain-language questions. Evidence requirements surface inline. Remediation steps personalized. Output: a one-page Council readiness summary, a detailed evidence pack for FMB, and a remediation plan with owners and dates.

For the Member

A Nation self-assesses in days rather than months. Confidence to engage FMB is materially higher.

For FMB

Capacity Development Managers receive submissions in a consistent, standards-mapped shape. Site visits focus on remediation, not orientation.

4

The Living Risk Register

From DOCX template (2019) to AI-augmented register
Today

A Word document. The Nation enumerates risks, ranks them, and writes mitigations — typically once a year, often perfunctorily.

Living

An AI-augmented register. The Nation answers contextual questions. The tool proposes a Nation-specific risk inventory from a library of typical First Nation risks. Mitigations suggested per risk and editable. Quarterly check-ins prompt review of only the risks that changed.

For the Member

Risk practice becomes continuous rather than annual. Council oversight improves.

For FMB

Standards team gains anonymized aggregate signal on which risk classes are systemically rising or falling.

5

The Living Annual Report Builder

From PPTX template to AI-augmented draft
Today

A PowerPoint template and a Word guidance document. A Nation manually drafts the annual report each year, typically without the time to do it well.

Living

The Nation uploads the audited financials, prior-year report, and 3–5 council direction notes. The tool produces a complete draft annual report — financial commentary, year-in-review narrative, FPC-aligned performance section, acknowledgements. The finance officer edits a near-finished document.

For the Member

Reduces annual report production from 60+ hours to 8–10. Quality and member engagement both rise.

For FMB

Aggregate aggregation across the membership becomes feasible — a national picture drawn from artefacts FMB already touches.

6

The Living TCA Companion

From XLS template (2020) to asset intelligence
Today

An Excel workbook with asset rows, classes, amortization, and a life-cycle plan. Manual updates each year. Reconciliation to the audit is its own project.

Living

An asset register with intelligence. New assets entered with AI-prompted classification. Life-cycle planning automated against asset class. Maintenance and replacement scheduling visible on a roadmap. Direct reconciliation to the audited financials.

For the Member

Capital planning becomes proactive, not reactive. Asset Maintenance Ratio improves naturally over time.

For FMB

DMPPP early-warning signals can be triangulated against asset deterioration trends across the membership.

§ III — Working Demonstrations

Three transformations, built and runnable. Today.

Three working, interactive demonstrations accompany this showcase. Each is a single HTML file — no installation, no connection. Open any one on a laptop, click a button, and feel what "living" means. The other three transformations will be built live with the FNFMB team during the working session itself.

Interactive Demonstrations · Open in any browser

Three demos. One visual language.

Each demonstration is rule-based and illustrative — FMB-aligned but not FMB-authoritative. In production, each would be a reasoning model with access to the Nation's audited financials, prior-year context, anonymized peer-Nation data, and FMB working papers — generating output dynamically rather than from a small built-in clause library.

§ IV — The Half-Day Working Session

Four programmed hours. Laptops on the table.

A working session, not a lecture. The format is collaborative; the screen is on; the FMB tools are on it. The session moves between demonstration, discussion, and live transformation.

0:00 – 0:20
Welcome
Territorial acknowledgement. Round-the-table introductions. The agenda. One question to set the room: which of the FMB tools, today, demands the most time from the people in this room? The answer shapes the second hour.
0:20 – 1:10
I. The Audit
Guided walkthrough of the Tools and Templates section as it exists today. Category by category — Governance, Information Management, Finance, HR, Local Revenues. For each, we name the static state, the user's experience, and the question: what would this become if it could think?
1:10 – 2:25
II. Six Transformations
Live, on-screen demonstration of each of the six transformations. Three of them deeper than the others, selected by the team during the morning. The Living FPC Coach is the anchor — the form is the same for the other five.
2:25 – 2:55
Break
Coffee. Informal conversation. The screen is left on.
2:55 – 3:55
III. The Internal Team
Three demonstrations focused not on member-facing tools but on what AI could do for FMB staff directly. Certification evidence pre-screen. Capacity Development cohort dashboard. Standards cross-jurisdiction research.
3:55 – 4:25
IV. The Roadmap Conversation
The honest question: if any of this should be built, how? Stage-and-prove approach. First three transformations in 90 days. Cost and timeline framework. Data sovereignty, hosting, and the question of who owns what.
4:25 – 4:55
V. Q&A
Open questions. Held back deliberately so the session does not have to budget for them inside the working time.
4:55 – 5:00
Close
Names what the next step would be — without asking for it. The proposal that follows is shaped by what this session reveals, not the other way around.
§ V — The Internal Team

What AI could do for the FMB team directly.

Member-facing tools are the lead example. They are not the whole story. The afternoon module focuses on what the FMB internal team could gain — directly, immediately, and without changing any FMB-facing artefact.

For Natalia's Team · Certification

Evidence Pre-Screen

Before the analyst opens a submission, AI has mapped each document to the relevant standard, flagged gaps, identified inconsistencies, and produced a one-page summary. The analyst's first 20 minutes are now spent on judgement, not on inventory.

Time saved: 1.5–3 hours per submission
Quality effect: Attention concentrates on hard cases
Standards effect: Patterns surface in aggregate
For Amy's Team · Capacity Development

Cohort Dashboard

Every Nation in a Manager's portfolio on one screen: latest ratios, certification status, latest engagement, last contact, flagged concerns, upcoming milestones. The Monday-morning view shows which calls matter most this week.

Time saved: 4–6 hours per Manager per week
Reach effect: Same team supports more Nations meaningfully
Equity effect: Quieter Nations surface in the dashboard
For Lee's Team · Standards

Cross-Jurisdiction Research

When the team considers a refinement to FMS, FPC, or FAL, an AI research companion compiles parallel literature in minutes: PSAB pronouncements, Indigenous fiscal frameworks (NZ, Australia, US tribal nations), peer-reviewed accounting research, and FMB historical submissions.

Time saved: 40–80 hours per major review
Quality effect: More comparative jurisdictions considered
Sovereignty: Closed-tenant cloud, no FMB data leaves perimeter
